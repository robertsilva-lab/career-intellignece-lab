Model methodology & limitations

January volume = 5,000. Each subsequent month = prior month × (1 + monthly growth). Revenue = units × price; contribution = revenue − units × variable cost; operating profit = contribution − fixed operating cost. The base case uses 1% growth, $120 price, $72 variable cost, and $150,000 monthly fixed cost. Breakeven units = fixed cost ÷ (price − variable cost), rounded up. No finite breakeven exists when contribution per unit is nonpositive and fixed costs are positive.

This simplified operating model excludes taxes, interest, depreciation, working capital, capital expenditures, price elasticity, and capacity constraints. It is not a three-statement model. Fractional forecast units are retained in calculations; displays are rounded.

Forecast method & interpretation

Each historical forecast uses only the preceding 3 or 6 months of actual demand. Both methods are evaluated over July–December for a fair comparison. WAPE = sum of absolute errors ÷ sum of actual units; bias = sum(forecast − actual) ÷ sum(actual). Positive bias means overforecasting. The January forecast uses the latest window of actuals × (1 + demand adjustment); that adjustment does not change the historical backtest.

Required hours = next-month demand × standard hours per unit. Capacity gap = available hours − required hours. The moving average is a baseline, not a seasonality-aware or statistically validated production model.

KPI definitions & data boundaries

Operating profit = revenue − variable costs − fixed operating costs. Operating margin = operating profit ÷ revenue. Revenue and profit variances = actual − budget; positive is favorable. For costs, negative variance is favorable. Margin variance is expressed in percentage points. Period totals are summed before calculating margin. The dashboard's monthly records are deterministic fictional examples, independent of the forecasting and demand datasets.
